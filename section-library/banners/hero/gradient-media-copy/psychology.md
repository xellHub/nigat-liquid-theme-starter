# Gradient Media Copy Psychology

Guidance for using immersive imagery, a directional scrim, and a bounded action area responsibly.

## Purpose

Explain when this hierarchy improves comprehension and when it becomes distraction, pressure, or decoration without meaning.

## Physical composition

The eye encounters full-frame media, moves into the high-contrast heading zone, scans supporting copy, and finishes at the action cluster.

## Psychology

The gradient protects reading without separating emotion from explanation. Subject placement draws attention toward clear media while the opaque edge stabilizes copy.

## Best used when

- Customers benefit from emotional orientation before evaluating one offer.
- Image and copy reinforce the same truthful claim.

**Decision rule:** Best used when visual context strengthens one truthful promise; not when imagery hides complexity or manufactures urgency.

## Avoid when

- Customers need immediate comparison among peer choices.
- The campaign relies on urgency language, inaccessible image text, or ambiguous actions.

## Good usage

A durable-clothing campaign shows material and fit while copy names the design principle and links to the represented collection.

## Bad usage

A generic lifestyle image paired with artificial urgency creates pressure without useful evidence. Replace it with accurate product context.

## Content requirements

- **Required:** A truthful relationship between image, claim, and destination.
- **Recommended:** Concrete benefit language and a predictable action.
- **Unsupported:** False scarcity, unsupported claims, or hidden limitations.

## Accessibility requirements

- The offer must remain understandable without seeing the image.
- Scrim and text must meet contrast requirements at every crop.
- The page must not depend on motion, sound, or image-embedded words.

## AI-agent guidance

- **Choose this when:** Visual evidence and one concise message jointly reduce uncertainty.
- **Reject this when:** The claim is unsupported, imagery is merely aspirational, or customers need equal comparison.
- **Prefer instead:** Product grids, rich text, or explicit split-panel variants.
- **Required evidence:** Campaign claim, image subject, destination relevance, decision stage, and contrast test.

## Attention sequence

1. Full-frame media establishes category, material, or mood.
2. The protected heading states the promise.
3. Supporting copy qualifies it before the primary action.

## Ethical safeguards

- Do not combine emotional imagery with fabricated scarcity or unverified claims.
- Keep the secondary action subordinate and make both destinations predictable.

## Audience fit

| Audience state           | Expected response                  | Appropriate? | Reason                                     |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | ------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| First-time visitor       | Understand the proposition         | Yes          | Media and concise copy provide orientation |
| Returning product seeker | Reach an assortment                | Sometimes    | Only when the action is direct             |
| Comparison shopper       | Compare peer options               | No           | Full-frame hierarchy hides equal choices   |
| Screen-reader user       | Understand the offer without media | Yes          | Semantic copy and links remain complete    |
