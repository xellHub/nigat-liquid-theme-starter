# Editorial Jumbo Text

An editorial family led by oversized typography.

## Purpose

An editorial family led by oversized typography.

## Physical composition

A dominant short statement occupies most of the visual field with restrained supporting content.

## Psychology

They create context and emotional continuity before asking for action. Narrative should clarify genuine value rather than conceal essential commercial facts. Within this family, variants must change how attention or interaction flows—not merely visual styling.

## Best used when

- a brief distinctive statement can carry the section without explanation.
- The surrounding page gives the composition enough context and space to perform its role.

**Decision rule:** Best used when a brief distinctive statement can carry the section without explanation; not when the copy is long, technical, legally important, or generic.

## Avoid when

- the copy is long, technical, legally important, or generic.
- A simpler section can communicate the same information with less interaction or visual weight.

## Good usage

A merchant uses this family when a brief distinctive statement can carry the section without explanation, then selects a child variant whose structure matches the available content.

## Bad usage

Using this family when the copy is long, technical, legally important, or generic creates unnecessary visual or interaction cost. Prefer editorial or rich-text.

## Content requirements

- **Required:** A coherent narrative progression and media that contributes information.
- **Recommended:** One idea per section, concise transitions, and an optional action after the story resolves.
- **Unsupported:** Decorative media sequences with no informational relationship or inaccessible autoplay.

## Accessibility requirements

- Keep narrative order identical in visual and document flow.
- Caption meaningful video and describe informative imagery.
- Provide controls for motion and respect reduced-motion preferences.

## AI-agent guidance

- **Choose this when:** a brief distinctive statement can carry the section without explanation, and at least one child variant matches the available content
- **Reject this when:** the copy is long, technical, legally important, or generic, or no concrete variant has evidence for the required interaction
- **Prefer instead:** editorial or rich-text.
- **Required evidence:** Content count, media shape, hierarchy, interaction model, viewport needs, and merchant-configurable data.
